The strategy is explicit about the EU's position: world-class research, fragmented implementation, and limited translation into market-ready products. The document's five pillars span the full quantum stack from basic research through skills development, with the infrastructure and space/dual-use pillars most operationally specific.

The infrastructure commitments include EuroQCI terrestrial and space-based quantum communication links, quantum chip pilot lines, and eventually an EU quantum foundry — investments that would directly support the board's communications and sensing constellations if delivered.

The Quantum Act, expected in Q2 2026, is intended to add binding measures on investment, industrial capacity, and supply chain security. Until that Act is proposed and adopted, this document records intent, not obligation.

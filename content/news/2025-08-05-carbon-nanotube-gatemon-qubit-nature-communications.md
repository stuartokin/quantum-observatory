The team used a hexagonal boron nitride substrate to achieve ultraclean nanotube integration, reducing interface disorder that limits coherence in dirtier systems. The qubit spectrum can be tuned with a gate voltage, reflecting the quantum dot character of the nanotube, and charge noise is identified as the dominant decoherence mechanism at this stage.

At 200 ns, this coherence is well below what is needed for fault-tolerant computation — existing transmons exceed 1 ms — but the significance is in the new material platform rather than the absolute coherence number. Carbon nanotubes are theoretically promising for quasiparticle-poisoning-resistant gatemons because of their one-dimensional structure; this is the first experimental demonstration that the concept works.

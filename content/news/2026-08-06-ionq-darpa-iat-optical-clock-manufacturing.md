The Evergreen-05 traces to DARPA's Robust Optical Clock Network programme, which funded Vector Atomic beginning in 2019. IonQ acquired Vector Atomic in October 2025 and inherited both the clock and the DARPA relationship.

The It's About Time programme is explicit that scaling quantum manufacturing — not improving the underlying physics — is the unsolved problem. No one has previously attempted to produce optical atomic clocks at this volume because integrating and miniaturising the laser and photonic components at scale has proved harder than the quantum physics itself. The $28M modification funds manufacturing development and 25 initial units; the $30M option, if exercised, would cover the remaining 100.

A dedicated production facility is expected to be operational by mid-2027. Deliverable units are expected within a year of that.

This is an announced programme with contractual delivery obligations, not a published result. The clocks have not yet been delivered, and the manufacturing line does not yet exist. The significance is in the procurement model: a defence agency treating quantum hardware as something to be produced in quantity on a schedule, rather than demonstrated.

Frolov et al. conducted four independent replication experiments in topological condensed matter physics — including experiments targeting Majorana zero modes in semiconductor-superconductor hybrid nanowires and other devices. In all four cases, the team found that limited datasets consistent with the presence of topological phenomena could be explained instead by trivial fine-tuning of device parameters. The broader dataset, when disclosed, pointed away from topological origins.

The paper argues that the field's practice of searching for a single dramatic 'smoking gun' signal, combined with limited data disclosure, creates conditions for systematic over-interpretation. The proposed remedy is comprehensive data sharing and publication of full parameter sweeps, not just the striking results.

This paper does not prove that topological qubits are impossible. It demonstrates that the evidentiary standards previously used to claim their detection are insufficient. The distinction matters: the path to topological quantum computing remains open, but the published evidence base for having started down it is formally weaker than it was before January 8, 2026.

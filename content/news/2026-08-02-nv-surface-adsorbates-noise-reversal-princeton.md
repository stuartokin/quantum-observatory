The result separates two frequency regimes. Removing adsorbates in ultrahigh vacuum worsens Hahn echo coherence time T2 by roughly a factor of four (low-frequency noise regime), but improves T1 in the ~100 MHz electric field noise regime. The authors attribute the low-frequency degradation to increased surface charge density in vacuum — surface charge that ambient adsorbates partially neutralise under normal conditions. These two regimes arise from distinct microscopic mechanisms.

For shallow NV sensing, which operates in the low-frequency noise regime for DC and low-frequency magnetometry, this finding suggests that aggressive surface cleaning aimed at adsorbate removal may be counterproductive. The result also highlights that prior assumptions about noise sources — that adsorbates are net harmful — were incomplete.

This is a preprint from a group with an established track record on NV surface physics. Independent replication is needed before surface preparation protocols for NV sensors should be revised.

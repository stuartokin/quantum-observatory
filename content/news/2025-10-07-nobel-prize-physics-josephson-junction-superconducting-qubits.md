The 1984–85 experiments used a superconducting loop with a Josephson junction cooled to near absolute zero. Clarke, Devoret and Martinis showed that the collective phase of billions of Cooper pairs — a macroscopic variable — could tunnel between quantum states and that the circuit's energy levels were quantised. This answered a fundamental question: quantum mechanics governs systems far larger than atoms.

Martinis later led Google's quantum computing team and the Sycamore processor that demonstrated quantum supremacy in 2019. Devoret is now Chief Scientist for Quantum Hardware at Google Quantum AI. The prize therefore bridges the foundational physics of the 1980s and the hardware programmes of today.

What this does not change: the award is for work four decades old. It confirms the physics underlying the dominant platform; it is not evidence that fault-tolerant quantum computing is closer than the hardware roadmaps already suggest.

The paper is a theoretical first: the authors propose the molybdenum-oxygen-vacancy complex [(MoZnvO)²⁺] as the first deep-level spin qubit candidate in ZnO and predict its spin and optical properties using ab initio methods. They also flag paramagnetic impurities as the dominant source of spin decoherence in ZnO — a practical concern for experimentalists who would attempt to fabricate such a device.

ZnO's advantages over diamond include a dilute nuclear spin background, compatibility with existing semiconductor foundry processes, and the ability to be grown as ultra-high-purity crystals at wafer scale. Whether the predicted properties survive fabrication and remain coherent at room temperature in a real device is an open experimental question.

This remains a prediction. The board should treat it as an emerging proposal, not an experimental result.

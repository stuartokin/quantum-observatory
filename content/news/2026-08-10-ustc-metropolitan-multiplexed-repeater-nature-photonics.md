The protocol introduced here — time-measurement multiplexing — allows photons to arrive at an intermediate station at different times, combining the high heralding rate of single-photon interference with the phase robustness of two-photon interference. The achieved entanglement distribution rate of 0.47 Hz at 78.6% fidelity is 127× higher than the best previous metropolitan result at comparable fidelity. The Bell inequality (CHSH) is violated by 3.7 standard deviations, certifying entanglement quality sufficient for device-independent protocols. The experiment uses two solid-state quantum memory nodes separated by 14.5 km of fibre; it is a single link and has not been extended to a multi-node network. The preprint version (arXiv:2508.17940) notes a follow-up study will examine scalability to global-scale networks.

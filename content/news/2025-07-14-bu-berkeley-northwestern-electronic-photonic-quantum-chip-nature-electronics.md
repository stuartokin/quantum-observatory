The chip integrates twelve microring resonator quantum photon-pair sources alongside electronic feedback circuits that continuously lock each resonator to its input laser. The platform uses a commercial 45 nm CMOS process, the same technology node used in production electronics, developed through a collaboration between BU, UC Berkeley, GlobalFoundries, and Ayar Labs.

What is demonstrated: reliable, scalable production of correlated photon pairs on a manufacturable platform with on-chip stabilisation. What is not demonstrated: a complete quantum computing operation, gate fidelity, error-corrected logical qubit, or quantum advantage over classical computation. The chip is a component — a quantum light source with its own control — not a processor. The photonic device design was led by Imbert Wang (BU) and circuit integration by Danielius Kramnik (UC Berkeley).

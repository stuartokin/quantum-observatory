The critical bottleneck for quantum repeaters has been that decoherence destroys entanglement faster than it can be established and purified at distances relevant to real networks. USTC's experiment uses trapped-ion memories with coherence times exceeding one second, combined with an efficient telecom-wavelength photon interface and a high-visibility single-photon entanglement protocol. The result crosses the threshold: entanglement survives longer than the time needed to create it over 10 km. As a practical application test, the team demonstrated device-independent QKD (DI-QKD) at 10 km, and computed a positive asymptotic key rate for 101 km — more than two orders of magnitude further than any prior DI-QKD experiment. The experiment uses spooled fibre in a laboratory, not deployed urban cable; operational conditions in the field would introduce additional noise sources.

The harmonium design combines the first and second harmonics of the Josephson potential to create a double-well energy landscape in which the two logical qubit states are spatially separated — a configuration that simultaneously suppresses bit-flip and phase-flip transitions. Earlier protected designs (cat qubits, 0-π qubits) achieve protection at the cost of either gate speed or fabrication complexity.

This is a theoretical paper. The gap between a proposed superconducting circuit design and a working qubit has historically been years and requires fabrication work to close. The paper is worth recording because it expands the design vocabulary for superconducting qubits and arrives in the same month the Nobel Prize reminded the field how directly this hardware lineage connects to fundamental physics.

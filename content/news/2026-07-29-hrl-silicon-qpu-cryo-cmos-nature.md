plain: 'The wiring problem has been one of the binding constraints on silicon spin qubit scaling: every qubit has historically required multiple control lines running from room-temperature electronics into the cryostat, making the number of cables grow with the number of qubits. HRL Laboratories has published a Nature paper showing a silicon quantum processing unit where all time-varying control signals are generated by a cryogenic CMOS chip operating at 4 K inside the same refrigerator. The 18-qubit system — built on a 54-quantum-dot array using standard 200mm silicon-germanium wafers and a commercial 130nm CMOS foundry — ran repeated rounds of error correction without real-time commands from room temperature, achieving a logical error rate 4.7 times lower at distance-5 than at distance-3. The result demonstrates that qubit chip, cryogenic controller, and interconnect can all be manufactured on standard semiconductor lines, providing a fabrication blueprint. The case for silicon is not today''s qubit count — it is the long-term manufacturing compatibility with the global semiconductor industry. IBM had announced its acquisition of HRL eight days before this publication.'

The system integrates three separately fabricated components: a 54-quantum-dot exchange-only qubit chip at millikelvin temperatures, a 70-million-transistor CMOS controller at 4 K, and a 296-channel superconducting niobium-on-polyimide ribbon cable connecting the two stages. Control errors were ten times lower than any prior exchange-only demonstration. The autonomy claim deserves precision: the cryogenic sequencer interprets written instructions and executes conditional branching, but initial tuning still requires human-guided calibration to known states; it does not independently handle arbitrary failures. The same issue of Nature carried a companion silicon spin-qubit paper from QuTech/Delft (Undseth et al., DOI 10.1038/s41586-026-10766-3) using a spin-shuttling approach to achieve weight-four parity checks — the two papers approach the silicon scaling problem from different directions.

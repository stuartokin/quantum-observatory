Project Farseer is organised into four Lines of Effort: (1) electric field sensors and magnetometers for weak-signal ISR; (2) gravity gradiometers for static, low-, and high-dynamics applications; (3) tactical clocks for precision navigation and timing; and (4) component technology insertions, covering chip-scale lasers, micro-optics, and silicon nitride photonic integrated circuits.

DIU is using Other Transactions Authority (OTA) contracting under 10 U.S.C. 4022. This means successful prototype contracts can convert directly to uncompetitive, large-scale follow-on production contracts without a new competitive award — an unusually fast procurement path by government standards. All submissions must comply with Sensor Open Systems Architecture (SOSA) standards, requiring integration with existing military platforms.

The timeline is aggressive: commercial prototypes ready for testing in three to nine months; a path to operational transition in two to three years. The $200M figure is an upper bound, not a guarantee — it depends on how many qualifying submissions the programme attracts and how prototype testing proceeds.

Farseer is not a research grant and not a laboratory exercise. It is the US military declaring that at least some quantum sensing modalities are close enough to useful field performance to be worth paying for now, in volume. For quantum sensing companies, this changes the near-term commercial calculation.
